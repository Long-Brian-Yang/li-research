| Priority | Paper | Why read it? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hu et al., Nature Communications (2023), [10.1038/s41467-023-39522-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39522-1) | Original experimental reference for the exact nominal composition |
| 2 | Zhang et al., JACS (2024), [10.1021/jacs.3c07343](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c07343) | Amorphous Zr oxychloride and local structural interpretation |
| 3 | Kim et al., Nature Communications (2025), [10.1038/s41467-025-65702-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-65702-2) | Nanocrystalline versus amorphous identification, and an AIMD comparison |

**Important:** the three papers do not study an identical composition or microstructure. A homogeneous melt-quenched simulation cell must not automatically be identified with a ball-milled experimental sample. None of these readings establishes that our particular MACE checkpoint is validated for melting or quenching this composition.

The reports are original summaries. No publisher PDF or figure is redistributed here. The two Nature Communications papers are CC BY 4.0; the JACS paper is linked, not reproduced.
